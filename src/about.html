How it started

My work begins with a simple question: what is happening underneath? With a computer science background, I’ve explored that question through projects that connect mathematical ideas to working software.

I started with the foundations of machine learning: implementing linear regression, gradient descent, and feature normalization. Building those pieces by hand made the relationship between data, model parameters, and predictions more tangible.

From there, I explored neural networks with TensorFlow and Keras. Projects such as handwritten digit classification and small classification models gave me a way to connect training pipelines with outputs I could inspect.

The next step was making those models useful through interfaces. My real estate prediction project connects a model built from scratch to Streamlit, so its inputs and predictions can be explored directly.

Sentinel AI takes that interest into LLM applications. It brings traces, evaluations, model comparisons, and retrieval experiments into a developer console. It’s a project about making the behavior of AI easier to inspect, including the limitations of the methods behind it.

At Futura Edtech, I work at the intersection of AI, software, and learning experiences. That combination keeps me interested in the whole journey: the underlying logic, the interaction, and the person using it.

There’s still plenty to learn. I keep returning to the same process: build a version, look closely at what it does, and use what I learn to improve the next one.
